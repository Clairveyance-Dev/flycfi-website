What this app is

FlyCFI is a record-keeping tool for flight instructors and their students. It holds a syllabus, lesson records, grades, flight time, endorsement wording and training documents.

What it is not

  • It is not the FAA, and it is not endorsed by or affiliated with the FAA.
  • It is not a substitute for 14 CFR, the ACS, the FAA handbooks, or a current chart.
  • It is not your logbook, and it is not your student’s logbook.
  • It does not decide whether anyone is ready to solo or to take a checkride. You do.

Where the app cannot work something out it says so; do not read silence as agreement.

The FAA library

FlyCFI carries the text of the FAA handbooks and the ACS so they can be searched and read in the app. That text is a snapshot, taken on the date shown against each book. It is believed accurate and it is not the official document: the FAA’s own PDF is, and every book in the library offers it.

The regulations, the AIM, the Pilot/Controller Glossary and the Advisory Circulars are deliberately not kept here, because they change too often for a copy to be safe. They open in FlyRegs instead, where they are kept current. An endorsement in particular must be copied from the current Advisory Circular and never from a saved PDF — you are the one who signs it.

Whichever you read, you remain responsible for checking the current regulation.

Your responsibility as an instructor

You are the certificate holder. Every endorsement you issue, every sign-off you make and every record you keep is yours, whatever this app shows. The guardrails in it are advisory: they tell you what is not yet met, and they never prevent you from exercising your own judgement — nor do they excuse it.

You are responsible for keeping the records 14 CFR 61.189 requires, and for keeping them for the three years the regulation asks. Keep your own copy. Print the record.
